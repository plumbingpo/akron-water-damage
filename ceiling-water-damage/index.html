Ceiling Water Damage Repair Help in Akron

A brown ring on the ceiling is the last thing to show up, not the first. Long before you see discoloration, the insulation is wet, moisture has followed the framing and a puddle sits on the hidden side of the panel. The leak may be a bathroom on the floor above, a pipe in the ceiling, an air conditioner drain line or the roof. We trace it to the source before anything gets patched.

Covering the mark with paint is not a repair. If the cavity above is still wet, the mark comes back and mold can start on the paper backing within 24 to 48 hours. The opening is kept as small as the damage allows, wet insulation comes out, joists and surrounding drywall are dried, and the area is patched, textured and painted to blend. Sagging changes the picture. Stay out from under it and call.

Signs to Look For

  • Small black or green specks appearing in a cluster overhead
  • Stale, mildew like odor you cannot trace to anything you can see
  • Drip marks at the top of a wall where it meets the ceiling
  • Bits of textured finish falling to the floor below a damp patch
  • Seams, tape lines and fastener heads becoming visible across the ceiling
  • A ceiling panel that droops, bulges or gives under light pressure
Behind the Surface

What Hides Behind It

The Leak Is Somewhere Else

The wet spot is often not below the leak, since water rides pipes, ducts and joists before it falls. Cutting in just under the mark may leave damp areas elsewhere untouched.

Older Ceiling Materials

Some older popcorn textures and plasters contain asbestos. Have a sample tested before any scraping, cutting or removal if your ceiling is one of these.

Damage on the Floor Above

The structure on the upper side, attic boards or the floor of the next story, tends to be wet too. Our inspection includes the space overhead, since moisture left there keeps feeding the problem.

Wet Lights and Wiring

Recessed lights, fans and junction boxes sit right in the wet zone. Turn off that room's breaker, because a wet fixture may short out.

Repair or Replace

What Can Be Saved

It depends on the material, how long it stayed wet and how clean the water was. We test before we decide, and we tell you why.

The Clock

The Damage Timeline

The first few hours

Hidden Travel Overhead

Water runs along joists and soaks into insulation. The drywall absorbs it from the back, so the room below may still look fine.

24 to 48 hours

Marks Start Showing

A stain forms and paint starts to blister. Mold can begin on the drywall paper and the wet insulation presses down on the panel.

Day 3 to 7

The Ceiling Bows

Drywall softens and pulls away from its screws. The ceiling bows between joists, seams open up and a musty odor reaches the room.

After a week

Sections May Fall

Saturated pieces may drop and carry soaked insulation down too. By now mold has settled into the space above, and the area needing repair has grown considerably.

Our Process

Our Ceiling Water Damage Repair Process

1

Trace the Leak

The first job is locating the starting point, which involves meter readings, a thermal camera and a look at whatever is over the ceiling. The leak has to be stopped before repairs make sense.

2

Make It Safe

We cut power to any wet fixture and move or cover the furniture underneath. If the ceiling is holding water, we release it in a controlled way and catch it.

3

Remove and Dry Out

Damaged drywall and wet insulation come out. The joists and nearby ceiling are dried with air movers plus a dehumidifier, and antimicrobial goes on wherever the water was dirty.

4

Repair and Finish

After the meter shows normal levels, new insulation and drywall go up, joints are taped and finished, the texture is matched and a stain blocking primer goes on ahead of paint.
