Roof Leak Water Damage in Akron: What to Know

Where you see the stain and where the rain gets in are usually two different places. Water enters at a raised shingle, a split in the flashing or a worn skylight seal, follows the rafters and decking downhill, and finally lands on the insulation. A ceiling stain tends to arrive late, well after the space overhead first got wet. We find the full wet area and dry it out.

Our trade is water damage restoration, and roofing is a separate one. Inside the home, our team takes out saturated insulation, dries the wood and drywall, treats any mold and repairs the ceiling. Should the leak still be active, our crew can cover that section with a tarp for now, or time our work with the roofing contractor you pick so nothing gets sealed over an open leak.

First Five Minutes

What to Do Right Now

  1. Cut Power to Wet FixturesWater coming through a ceiling fan, a light or close to an outlet means that circuit should be turned off at the panel, and the fixture should not be touched.
  2. Catch the DripsPut a pan or bucket beneath each drip, and shift rugs, electronics and furniture clear of the wet area. Lay towels or plastic sheeting down to protect the floor.
  3. Relieve a Ceiling BulgeWhen water pools in a bubble overhead, pierce the low point with a screwdriver while standing clear, and let it run into a bucket.
  4. Leave the Roof AloneA wet roof and an unlit attic are both places to stay out of. Note from inside where the water is coming in and leave the rest to professionals.
  5. Document What You SeeTake photos and video of the ceiling, the attic and any damaged belongings before you move or throw anything away, then call us to start drying.
Will Insurance Pay?

What About Insurance?

When a leak happens suddenly and by accident, for example a branch going through the roof or wind stripping shingles, many homeowners policies pay for the water damage inside. A leak put down to an old, worn or neglected roof is usually denied, and fixing the roof may be treated separately from the interior work. Photos and moisture readings from us go to the adjuster, though only your policy decides coverage, and your agent can explain it.

Common Causes

Why This Happens

  • Nails that have backed out of the decking and left small holes through the shingles.
  • A fallen limb or large hail that punctures or bruises the roof covering.
  • Gutters packed with leaves, which push water back beneath the first row of shingles.
  • Rubber boots around plumbing vent pipes that split after years in the sun.
  • Leaves and grit collecting where two roof slopes meet, damming rain until it seeps through.
What Is at Risk

What the Water Damages

Ceiling Drywall

A ceiling panel left damp will discolor, bow and weaken. Joints open, tape peels, and a waterlogged piece may fall without much notice.

Rafters and Decking

Repeated wetting makes decking, trusses and rafters turn dark, swell up and go soft. Decay here means less strength in what carries the roof.

Hidden Attic Mold

Mold does well in an attic that is warm, dark and full of damp wood and drywall paper. It may take hold in 24 to 48 hours and work its way over the underside of the sheathing.

Insulation in the Attic

Once soaked, insulation flattens, loses its value and keeps the drywall under it damp for days. Fiberglass can sometimes be dried. Cellulose that is saturated generally comes out.
