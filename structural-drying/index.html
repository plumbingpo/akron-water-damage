No part of restoring a water-damaged building matters more than structural drying, yet it is also where untrained crews make the most mistakes. Extraction takes care of standing water, but drywall, framing, subflooring, insulation, concrete and other building materials stay loaded with trapped moisture. A structure left wet ends up with odors, wood rot, mold and failing materials, so the moisture has to be removed in a scientific way using dehumidification and controlled evaporation.

Akron Restoration Co employs IICRC-certified structural drying technicians who understand psychrometry, the study of temperature, humidity and moisture relationships in air. Using this knowledge, they calculate the specific equipment placement, temperature settings and airflow patterns needed to create the right evaporation conditions for each drying situation. Different materials (drywall, hardwood, concrete, plaster) dry at different rates and need different approaches to reach acceptable moisture levels without causing additional damage.

Every day of the drying process, we check moisture using thermal imaging cameras, thermo-hygrometers and calibrated moisture meters. The result is a detailed log of readings that tracks how moisture comes down over time in each material that got wet. Those readings guide daily equipment adjustments that keep the drying as efficient as it can be. Drying is complete only when all affected materials reach acceptable moisture standards as defined by IICRC S500 guidelines, not when surfaces simply feel dry to the touch.

Why Professional Structural Drying Matters

There is real science behind structural drying, which is why guessing does not work. Secondary mold damage most often traces back to someone who put down fans and hoped things would dry. It involves daily monitoring, a calculation of the required grain depression (the capacity to remove moisture) and a working knowledge of the way materials absorb moisture and let it go. Lacking that understanding, people either stop too soon and leave moisture for mold, or dry too hard and harm wood and other materials.

Professional drying equipment is not a bigger version of what stores sell, it is a different kind of machine. Commercial LGR (low-grain refrigerant) dehumidifiers remove 10 to 20 times more moisture from the air than residential dehumidifiers, and high-velocity air movers create the precise airflow patterns needed to accelerate evaporation from structural surfaces. This equipment represents a significant investment and requires training to deploy effectively, both reasons why professional drying produces far better outcomes than DIY attempts.

How to Prevent Structural Drying Problems

Taking preventive measures can help you avoid costly water damage issues down the road. Here are our expert recommendations:

  • Tell your restoration technician immediately if you notice new wet areas or odors during drying
  • The dehumidifiers and air movers should run around the clock until our technician takes them out
  • Dehumidifiers lose efficiency when outside air gets in, so keep drying areas closed up
  • Plan for daily access so readings can be taken and equipment adjusted
  • Air movers need a clear path, so avoid stacking things on the equipment or in front of it
  • Go by the meter, not by touch, since a dry-feeling surface may still hold moisture deep inside

How to Tell You Need Structural Drying

Recognizing the warning signs early can save you time, money, and prevent significant damage to your property. Here are the most common indicators that it's time to call a professional:

  • Wood flooring with boards that cup, crown or pull apart
  • Window glass that fogs up near the affected rooms
  • Wall finishes that start to peel or bubble in the days after water damage
  • Surfaces that stay cool or clammy once the standing water has been removed
  • Materials breaking down in a spot that got wet before and was not dried by professionals
  • Swollen trim and baseboards separating at the seams
  • Drywall that is soft, swollen or shows moisture staining

How Our Structural Drying Works

Our systematic approach delivers quality results on every project. Here's how we handle structural drying from start to finish:

1

Moisture Assessment

Hygrometers, moisture meters and a thermal camera let us map moisture across each area that was affected. From that map we decide where equipment goes and record our baseline readings.

2

Equipment Deployment

Commercial LGR dehumidifiers and high-velocity air movers are strategically positioned based on our moisture map. Temperature and airflow are optimized for the specific materials being dried.

3

Daily Checks and Adjustments

Our technician returns daily to take moisture readings at all monitoring points, adjust equipment placement and document drying progress. We keep you posted on how the timeline looks.

4

Verifying the Dry Standard

When moisture readings reach acceptable levels throughout all affected materials, we document final readings, remove equipment and certify that your property has reached dry standard.
