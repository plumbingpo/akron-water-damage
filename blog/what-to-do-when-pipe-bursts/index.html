Burst Pipe Emergency Guide in Akron, OH

Home / Blog / What to Do When a Pipe Bursts in Akron, OH

A burst pipe is one of the most stressful plumbing emergencies a homeowner can experience. Water pours out of a burst line at a rate of 4 to 8 gallons a minute, so within hours rooms may be flooded, possessions damaged, and building materials weakened. What you do in the first few minutes after finding the break often decides whether you face a simple repair or a full restoration, and it can keep thousands of dollars of damage off the bill. Akron Restoration Co put together these steps so Akron, OH residents know exactly how to respond to this common problem.

Step 1: Shut Off the Water Supply Immediately

Before anything else, cut off the water, because nothing you do afterward matters as much as stopping the flow quickly. Damage and repair bills grow with each minute the leak runs. Start by finding the main shutoff valve for the house. In most Akron homes, it sits near the water meter, typically in the garage, basement, utility room, or along an exterior wall near the street. Turn the valve clockwise (righty-tighty) to shut off water flow to the entire house. Some lines have their own local shutoff, so if you can tell which pipe failed, turn off that valve also.

If you cannot locate or operate your shutoff valve, call your water utility immediately, since they can shut off water at the meter. This is one of the most important reasons to know your shutoff valve location BEFORE an emergency. Take a few minutes today to locate yours and make sure it operates smoothly. A valve that will not move should be repaired by a plumber now, not during a flood.

Step 3: Protect Your Property and Belongings

While waiting for professional help, take these steps to minimize damage: move valuable items, electronics, and documents away from wet areas. Slip plastic blocks or aluminum foil beneath furniture legs so nothing sits directly on soaked carpet. Take area rugs off floors that got wet. Where puddles are easy to get to, blot them up with towels. Open closet and cabinet doors to promote air circulation. If you can reach the breaker panel safely and stay out of the water, switch off power to the affected rooms.

Do not step into pooled water anywhere close to outlets, electrical panels, or appliances. If the burst pipe has caused significant flooding and you cannot safely access your electrical panel, leave the electricity on and wait for professionals to assess the situation. Your safety matters more than the house.

Step 4: Take Photos Before You Clean Up

Hold off on cleanup until you have a thorough record of the damage to give your insurer. Shoot video and photos showing the standing water, every damaged material, and any belongings that were hit. Write down when you found the break, both the date and the rough time, and photograph the leak's source if you can see it. Akron Restoration Co supplies detailed reports as well, and paired with your notes they strengthen your claim and your case for fair payment.

Preventing Burst Pipes in Akron

Insulating bare pipes in crawl spaces, garages, attics, and other unheated areas is a big step Akron homeowners can take, even though no measure prevents every burst. A slight drip from your faucets in a cold snap keeps the water in motion. Warm room air should be able to reach pipes on exterior walls, so open the cabinets under those sinks. Monitor your water pressure and install a pressure reducing valve if pressure exceeds 80 PSI. Braided stainless steel should take the place of worn supply lines, rubber washing machine hoses above all. Finally, learn where the shutoff valve is ahead of time.
